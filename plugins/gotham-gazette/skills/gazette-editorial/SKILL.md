# Write from inside the league

Read [house rules](../gazette-weekly/references/house-rules.md), [league bible](../gazette-weekly/references/league-bible.md), and the preceding published issue. Use only verified packet facts for sports claims.

Open with the strongest league story and Pat’s requested angle. Make NFL events matter through Gotham results instead of retelling the NFL game. Use roughly 60% analysis, 30% smart comedy, and 10% nonsense at an eighth- to ninth-grade reading level. Write short paragraphs, familiar language, earned jokes, understatement, and real callbacks. Avoid AI declarations, grand metaphors, repetitive punchlines, and explaining the joke. Use American spelling and no em dashes. Evoke sharp observational comedy without imitating a living writer’s exact voice.

Use team names in headlines and approved public manager labels in stories. Cover all six matchups and give all 12 teams at least one substantive observation, beyond listing them in a table. Do not favor Loose Endz or use Pat as the default example of good management. Vary recap length with importance. Treat known relationships as context for the joke, not the news itself.

Include the lead, results, six recaps, a 12-team board, Chaos Desk with numeric index and scale, bench receipts where supported, meaningful transactions, current waiver/news coverage where supported, and confirmed next-week matchups. Surface archives, awards, coaching, and corrections when there is evidence. Reserve Editor’s Note for a real reason. Allow special formats when Pat requests them.

Clearly label normal power rankings as editorial competitive judgments, distinct from Yahoo standings. Explain evidence for significant moves. For a special Chaos Agents or rooting-interest board, state what is ranked and that it is not competitive power or official standings; honor requested placements and do not invent ordinary movement arrows. Keep actual results and standings accurate even during fictional editorial feuds.

Use themes for the current issue only unless renewed. Remove expired UK, witches, holiday, or election framing from the next edition. Carry forward a feud or callback only when the new result or user direction earns it. Keep the George/editor rivalry affectionate and playful, never genuinely hostile. For controversial history, put jokes on the responsible actors rather than victims. Treat real roster actions as fair league play, never imply the publication executed a transaction.

Check each numeric claim against the computed output. If the story changes after writing, rerun the affected facts, captions, board, metadata, and art checks. Deliver a cohesive issue in the existing newspaper design, not just an outline, image prompt, or unrelated dashboard.
